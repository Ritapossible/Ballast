What Ballast is

Ballast is an autonomous overnight risk desk for tokenized US equities. Every evening it looks at each position you hold, judges whether tonight can move it, and - when it can - buys protection that removes almost all of the night's price risk. Every call it makes is graded against reality the next morning.

It is priced protection, not alpha. Ballast makes no claim to improve returns and no Sharpe claim. It removes variance for a stated price, on nights where that trade is worth making.

The problem

The US primary market is open 6.5 hours a day, five days a week - 32.5 of every 168 hours. For the other ~81% of the week a tokenized stock keeps trading while the market that prices its underlying is closed.

Companies report earnings after the close by design. The Fed moves mid-afternoon and the consequences play out overnight. Geopolitics arrives at weekends. All of it lands while a holder is exposed and cannot act in the underlying.

Today that holder has two options: sell before the close and give up the position they actually want, or hold and absorb whatever arrives. Measured overnight moves in this sample reach 1,262 bp in a single night.

Who it is for

Conviction holders of tokenized US equities - someone holding one to ten positions for weeks or months, 500-50,000 USDT apiece, directionally bullish and drawdown-averse, who will not exit before a catalyst because exiting defeats a multi-month thesis.

Their alternatives fail specifically: exiting a position costs 20 bp round trip and surrenders it; there are no options on rTokens; and the underlying market is shut, so it cannot be hedged there.

The hedge

Every rToken with a perp leg has a matched stock perpetual trading on the same 24/7 clock. Shorting that perp against the token cancels almost all of the overnight move.

  • Median R² 0.980 across 12 names, 100-260 nights each, with β within 4% of 1.00 - close to a one-for-one hedge.
  • It strengthens under stress. R² is 0.978-0.999 on top-decile move nights and 0.77-0.96 on calm ones. On a big-news night the common factor dominates and both instruments track it almost exactly; on a quiet night the residual is venue microstructure noise. The hedge is loosest only when little is at stake.
  • Median 88% reduction in the p95 tail. MSFT's worst night falls from 1,128 bp to 233 bp; AMD's from 1,262 bp to 90 bp.
  • Cost 11.3 bp taker round trip, net of funding received on the short. Cheaper than the 20 bp it costs to exit - and you keep the position.
  • Crypto is not a hedge. Median R² against BTC is 0.114. Crypto legs are excluded by measurement, not preference.

Of 1173 live rTokens, 241 have a perp leg (measured 2026-09-11). Ballast states plainly which positions it cannot protect rather than pretending otherwise.

Choosing the night

Because a delta hedge is symmetric - it removes upside with downside - value exists only on nights carrying variance without compensation. Two selectors were tested against that bar.

SelectorSeparation Compensated?Verdict
Trailing realised volatility1.41× yes - +19.3 bp, t=3.08unusable
Earnings calendar3.2× no - -61 bp, t=-1.59the selector

Volatility barely distinguishes a risky night from an ordinary one, and the nights it picks carry positive expected return - so hedging them pays to remove return. Earnings nights separate three times better and carry no reliable compensation. Earnings-night 1σ is 392 bp against a 11.3 bp cost, roughly 35:1.

This is why the model is necessary. The statistical selector demonstrably fails, and the calendar reaches only part of the tail - in replay it covered 2 of the 6 worst position-nights. Macro shocks, guidance, legal rulings and product events drive the rest and appear on no calendar. Reading them requires reading text.

The decision policy

Each evening, per position:

  • The calendar is checked for a scheduled report inside the window.
  • The event reader is given the night's headlines and the calendar flag, and returns HEDGE, NO_HEDGE or ABSTAIN.
  • Its judgment leads. On abstention or a failed gate, the deterministic calendar rule decides instead.
  • The volatility gate ships off. Re-enabling it requires new evidence, not a hunch - the measurement above is encoded in the code comment.

Architecture

CALENDAR   scheduled events - Nasdaq, serves historical dates
READER     Qwen qwen3.8-max, temperature 0 · headlines + calendar flag
           -> HEDGE | NO_HEDGE | ABSTAIN, with sources
ENGINE     deterministic: risk vs cost, under the signed mandate
ENFORCER   separate process, only write-scoped key, sees no reasoning
EXECUTOR   Bitget SDK, paper mode
LEDGER     append-only, hash-chained, signed
SETTLEMENT graded at the primary open against the exact counterfactual

Sources are deliberately keyless where possible: Nasdaq for scheduled events, Google News RSS per ticker for unscheduled ones, filtered to the window being decided so a headline published after the open cannot inform a decision taken before it.

Bounded authority

Ballast cannot place a bet. Every order it is structurally capable of emitting is opposite in sign to, and bounded in size by, a spot position already held. There is no code path to a directional trade.

The model owns the judgment and nothing else - there is no size, side, price or quantity field anywhere in its output contract, and a test asserts their absence. Three gates stand between it and an order:

  • Schema - the answer parses into the contracted shape, or it is refused.
  • Identity - a judgment about a different ticker is never accepted.
  • Grounding - the quoted headline must actually appear in the supplied sources. This is what stops a fabricated source reaching the book.

The enforcer runs as a separate process holding the only write-scoped credential. It never sees the model's reasoning; it checks arithmetic against a signed mandate - a position exists, the order opposes it, the size is within the ratio, the symbol is in the universe, the mandate has not expired, the night's caps are intact. 18 red-team tests drive hostile intents at it, each asserting the specific rule that refused it.

The ledger

Every decision, including every refusal, is appended to a hash-chained, signed ledger. Mutating, deleting, reordering or forging an entry breaks verification, and tests cover each of those attacks.

A scheduled job writes it and commits it, so each decision is recorded before its outcome is known and timestamped independently. The chain proves nothing was edited; the commit history proves nothing was backfilled.

Settlement

At the next primary open every decision is graded against the exact counterfactual - what the position would have done unhedged is not modelled, it is observed on the same window.

  • Win rate is the share of hedges that reduced the move. A hedge is symmetric, so a P&L-direction win rate would be meaningless.
  • Tail coverage is the share of the worst 1% / 5% / 10% of position-nights that were hedged - the metric the product should be judged on.
  • A session is never settled partially. Marking it done while a row is still ungradeable would strand that row permanently, so settlement waits for the whole session.

The first policy replay is published as a negative result: a volatility-led policy hedged 45% of nights, cost roughly 13% a year and turned a 1.11% return into -6.96%. Switching to calendar-led selection cut the hedge rate to 2.4% and the drag to 0.3 bp per position-night, with 15 of 16 hedges reducing the move - but coverage of the worst nights remains the open problem.

Defects found

Published rather than quietly corrected, because a project that reports only its successes has told you nothing about its error rate.

  • DST - the US close was hardcoded at 20:00 UTC while four months of sample were EST. Fixed; both transitions are pinned by test.
  • Look-ahead - the first neutrality test selected nights by realised move. Fixed; the invalid version survives behind a flag to document it.
  • Hour-snapping - the 09:30 ET open does not fall on an hourly bar boundary, so price lookups silently missed. Fixed.
  • A holiday counted as a trading day - the function that picks the session to trade tested only for a weekday, while the rest of the calendar excludes holidays. On Thanksgiving it returned Thanksgiving: a session the exchange never opened, against a window that never existed. Fixed and moved beside the calendar it has to agree with.
  • Measured figures were still being typed - MSFT's and AMD's worst nights appeared as literals in three places across the two page builders, and the whole out-of-sample paragraph was typed on the day it was measured, while both sat in docs/facts.json the entire time. That is the failure the universe counts had, in the files that exist to prevent it. Both render from the measurement now, and a test fails on any typed pair. The claim table was worse: it asserted "18 red-team tests" against a file holding 25, and "100-260 nights" against a sample running to 264 - counts are counted now.
  • The settled table hid the number that makes it checkable - it showed Unhedged, Realised and Value added, but value added is realised minus the counterfactual, and that column was not on the page. Unhedged is the same figure as the counterfactual on a hedge and the same figure as realised on a refusal, so every row printed one number twice and one that could not be derived from anything visible: a refusal read +439, +439 and +440. The column is now Counterfactual, so every row is a subtraction a reader can do, and a test rejects any row where it does not hold.
  • A declined position showed a notional - the Tonight table labelled the column "Notional USDT" and printed the position size on every row, including the ones where nothing was traded. On a page whose whole claim is that most nights it does nothing, a row reading NO_HEDGE beside 999 invites exactly the wrong conclusion. The same number now says which it is: hedged, or exposed.
  • The look-ahead sentinel could not fail - the evidence page cited a test that fails if a future night moves a past decision. That test built a polluted history and then passed the original to the policy both times, comparing a function against itself on identical input; its one use of the polluted list was an assertion about the list's length, which is why nothing flagged the variable as dead. The property also is not one of the policy, which is a pure function of whatever history it is handed - look-ahead can only enter where the history is sliced. The sentinel now corrupts every night at or after the session and requires the decision not to move, with two companions proving it can fail: the same corruption applied to earlier nights does move it, and widening the slice from strictly-before to inclusive moves it too.
  • Three tests were silently disabled - two test classes shared a name, so the second shadowed the first and the suite ran 216 of 219 while reporting success. The lost ones covered the disclosure that a session was decided too late to be ex-ante. Found by the linter within a minute of adding one.
  • A missed night was unrecoverable and unnoticed - the decide run had one scheduled trigger, and GitHub treats schedules as best effort: this repository's were late by 1h23m, 1h52m and 3h17m inside a single week. A settlement can catch up, because it grades whatever is outstanding; a decision cannot, because it is graded on having been written before the outcome was known. The job also reported success for having started, so a run that produced nothing looked exactly like one that worked. The decide half now has three independent triggers - deciding twice is already a no-op, so a backup can only rescue a night - and a coverage check fails the job when a session that was due has no decision on the chain.
  • A failed news feed looked like a quiet night - every exception from the headline fetch became an empty list, so a DNS failure, an HTTP 500, a timeout and "nothing was published" were indistinguishable. The reader would abstain on an empty brief for what looked like a legitimate reason, which means the model half of the product could have degraded across every position for days without a single signal. The fetch now raises a typed failure, the ledger records the reason on the row, and the night summary counts how many positions lost the feed.
  • The safety claim was caller-enforced, not structural - the executor took a symbol, a side and a size, so the only thing preventing a naked directional order was that the night run checked the verdict first. A new code path that forgot would have placed one, and the eighteen red-team tests could not have caught it: they drive the enforcer, and the gap was downstream of it. The executor now accepts only an Admitted, which the enforcer alone can mint and only for an intent opposite in sign to, and bounded by, a position already held. A caller that skips the enforcer has nothing to pass.
  • The README quoted stale figures - 224 of 704 rTokens, when the measured universe was 241 of 1,173. The one file exempt from the rule against literals was the one most people read. Its figures are generated from docs/facts.json now, and CI fails if they drift.
  • The landing page hid its own hedges - the live widget listed the first five positions in book order, and since the policy declines about ten nights in twelve those five were all refusals. The two hedges sat seventh and eighth and were never shown, so the one element meant to prove the system is working read as a system doing nothing. Hedges now lead, and the five rows carry a count so they cannot misrepresent twelve.
  • Tables looked empty on a phone - a 560px table on a 400px screen parks its last column off-screen, but that column's text still sets the row height. The Reasoning sentence produced 450px-tall rows on the Tonight page showing two short cells and a wall of blank. The data was there, just where it could not be read. Every table now stacks into labelled rows below 720px, and a layout test fails if a cell ships without its label.
  • The calendar selector hedged a night early - the rule matched the session date or the next session's and ignored the release time. Nasdaq lists a report under the date it is released, and only two of the four combinations fall inside a close-to-open window: after-hours on the session, or pre-market on the next one. An after-hours report on the next session was matched anyway, so the night before every such event was hedged as well as the night itself - two nights of cost per event, one of them protecting nothing. It is how the 2026-09-09 session hedged ORCL, which reported the following night. The hedge cut the move regardless, because a hedge cuts whatever arrives, but the reason recorded for it was wrong. Fixed; the settled page carries the correction beside the affected rows. Historical dates carry no release-time flag, so they still match on date alone - the rule Gate 1 measured - and the replay now calls the same selector rather than keeping its own copy.
  • The night could be decided twice - settlement had been made idempotent during the audit, but the decide half had not. A second run for the same session appended a second full set of decisions, committed a second mandate's worth of notional against one book, and made settlement grade every position twice. The workflow serialises simultaneous runs but not sequential ones, so a manual run followed by a cron GitHub delayed by hours was enough. A repeat is now a no-op rather than an error, so a late scheduled run is not reported as a failure for correctly declining to duplicate work.
  • A session decided after its own window - a manual run taken while testing re-decided the 2026-09-09 session at 12:45 the following day: 16.8 hours after the close, 45 minutes before the market reopened. Settlement grades close-to-open, so it credited that hedge with covering a move that had already happened, and nothing in the record said so. The entry stays in the chain - removing it is exactly the edit the design exists to prevent - and the Tonight page now states its lag and that it is not an ex-ante decision. The night run refuses a window more than half elapsed unless forced, and every run records how long after the close it was taken. Scheduled runs are often hours late, so the bound is generous: half of a 17.5 hour window is over eight hours of slack.
  • Settlement never ran unattended - the market cache directory is excluded from the repository, so on a fresh runner its parent did not exist and the create call raised. It could not fail locally, and the tests mock the market client, so every scheduled settlement failed silently from the day the loop was automated. Fixed; the client now has its own tests, and the pages state how many sessions behind they are so the next silent stoppage is visible here.
  • Broken metrics, twice - an early "decision accuracy" compared move against cost on every night, scoring nearly every unhedged night as an error. Its replacement, signed value added, is the right number, but it was still rendered as a per-night correct/wrong on refusals - and a refusal's value added is positive exactly when the position rose, which is a directional verdict this system does not make. A refusal now carries its arithmetic and no verdict; a hedge is graded on whether it cut the move, which is symmetric. Both fixed.

Verify it yourself

One command, no key and no network. It re-derives what can be re-derived offline and names what it cannot, rather than claiming to verify a figure it did not compute.

git clone https://github.com/Ritapossible/Ballast && cd ballast
python3 verify.py

It runs the suite, drives a naked directional order at the enforcer and requires a refusal, re-derives the ledger's hash chain, mutates a copy of a ledger and requires verification to fail, checks every published figure comes from docs/facts.json rather than a literal, confirms each documented research script exists, and builds all five pages. The market measurements need the exchange, so it points at the scripts that reproduce those instead of pretending otherwise.

Out of sample

Every other figure on the site is fitted on all the nights it covers. The hedge ratio is also tested held-out: beta estimated on the first 70% of each name's paired nights and applied unchanged to the last 30%, with no refit. Median variance removed goes 0.980 to 0.996, median p95 tail cut 86% to 94%, and the median absolute beta drift on refitting is 0.009 - 12 of 12 names hold.

The out-of-sample figures come out higher. That is a property of the period, not evidence the hedge improved; read it as stable rather than as better. The claim being tested is only that a mechanism does not decay on data it never saw, which is what separates a hedge from an edge. Reproduce with python3 research/oos.py.

Roadmap

What is still open, why, and what it would take. Ordered by how much it would change the product rather than by effort. What is not planned is listed too, so an absence reads as a decision rather than an oversight.

1 · Does the reader close the tail-coverage gap?

The open question the whole thesis rests on. The calendar selector covered 2 of the 6 worst position-nights in replay. Macro shocks, guidance, legal rulings and product events drive the rest, and the reader exists to reach them - but that has not been measured.

It cannot be measured retrospectively without look-ahead: judging a past night needs the headlines as they stood before the open, and no free source serves a point-in-time archive. So it accumulates forward.

  • Needs roughly 30 sessions of live reader decisions, then the tail-coverage cohorts re-run split by who decided.
  • Blocked by wall-clock time. A handful of sessions exist.
  • Until then the site says coverage is incomplete rather than implying otherwise.

2 · Live execution through the Agentic Account

Fills are simulated against observed prices with the real fee schedule, and the ledger is exported in Bitget UTA order field names so it reads alongside a real log - but no order reaches an exchange. The executor interface is already the seam: a live implementation replaces one class and nothing upstream changes.

  • Needs OAuth credentials for a Bitget Agentic sub-account.
  • Blocked by credentials, which are the operator's to issue.
  • Risk if rushed - an untested live-order path is worse than an honest simulated one.

3 · Position-level reporting

The replay measures a twelve-name equal-weight book, which is already diversified, so its worst night is a market-wide move that hedging some names barely dents. The target user holds one to ten positions. Position-level tail cohorts are computed but not yet surfaced. Nothing blocks this; it is the next substantive feature.

4 · The rTokens with no perp leg

Two thirds of the listed universe cannot be hedged at all. Ballast reports which, and stops there. A correlated proxy with a tradeable leg could cover some of them, with the residual basis risk reported honestly - but a proxy hedge is a different product with a different error profile, and claiming one without measuring it is exactly what the defects list exists to prevent.

5 · Maker execution

Every cost assumes taker on both legs. Maker pricing would be roughly a third of that, which materially widens the set of nights worth hedging. It is deliberately not assumed until the fill rate in a 4am perp book has been measured: an unfilled hedge is worse than an expensive one.

6 · Richer sources for the reader

The reader sees news headlines. Earnings calls, filings and the macro calendar are richer signals it does not read. The grounding gate already accepts any source, since it only checks that a quote appears in what was supplied.

Not planned

  • Predicting direction. Earnings nights measured as not reliably compensated; the product removes variance and makes no directional claim.
  • A volatility-based selector. Measured at 1.41× separation on compensated nights. The gate exists in the code and ships disabled; re-enabling it needs new evidence, not a hunch.
  • Real capital. Out of scope for this build.

Reproduce it

git clone https://github.com/Ritapossible/Ballast && cd ballast
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests   # full suite, no network, no key
python3 research/hedge_study.py         # the hedge measurements
python3 research/gate1_selection.py     # why volatility fails
python3 research/gate1_calendar.py      # why the calendar works
python3 research/replay.py              # the policy over history
python3 research/costs_study.py         # every fee and funding figure
python3 research/facts_study.py         # re-measure the universe counts

CLI

python3 -m ballast.bootstrap   build the paper position book
python3 -m ballast.night       decide, enforce, execute, record
python3 -m ballast.morning     settle against the counterfactual
python3 -m ballast.report      rebuild this site
python3 -m ballast.export      export the log in Bitget order schema
python3 -m ballast.preflight   check credentials without writing
python3 -m ballast.universe    list the hedgeable universe

--dry-run records decisions without fills. --no-reader runs the deterministic calendar rule alone.
